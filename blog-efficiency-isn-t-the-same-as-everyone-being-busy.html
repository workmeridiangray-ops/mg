Efficiency isn't the same as everyone being busy

5 min read · Meridian Gray

A business can look highly active — everyone occupied, phones ringing, inboxes full — and still be fundamentally inefficient, because busyness measures effort, not whether that effort is going toward the work that actually matters.

Why busy can mask inefficiency

Manual, repetitive tasks that could be automated keep people genuinely occupied, which makes the business feel productive even when a meaningful share of that activity is just working around a process gap that was never fixed. The busyness is real. It's also, in part, evidence of the underlying problem, not proof that the problem doesn't exist.

A better question than "how busy is the team"

Ask what proportion of a typical day goes toward tasks that directly move a customer, an order, or a decision forward, versus tasks that exist only to compensate for a process that doesn't connect properly — re-entering data, chasing updates, manually checking something a system should confirm automatically.

What real efficiency looks like

Not a frantic pace — often the opposite. A genuinely efficient business frequently looks calmer than a busy but inefficient one, because less effort is being spent on compensating for gaps, and more of the team's attention is available for the work that actually requires judgement.

Our research phase specifically looks for effort being spent on compensating for process gaps — that's usually where the real efficiency gain is hiding, not in asking people to simply work faster.
